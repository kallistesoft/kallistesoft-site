Flutter vs native (Swift/Kotlin): how to choose in 2026

By Kallistesoft · November 28, 2026 · 3 min read

For ten years I've shipped apps to Google Play and the App Store in Swift, Kotlin, Unity and, lately, mostly Flutter. "Flutter or native?" comes up on every project. Here are the criteria I actually use.

What changes in practice

Native means two codebases: Swift (SwiftUI) for iOS and Kotlin (Jetpack Compose) for Android. Flutter is one Dart codebase that draws its own components. The UI is identical and smooth everywhere, but it doesn't automatically pick up every new iOS or Android visual change.

1. The team first

A solo developer or a team of two to four who must cover iOS and Android almost always benefits from Flutter: a feature is written and tested once. If you already have solid iOS and Android teams, forcing Dart on them to "save money" rarely pays off. Pick the technology your team can maintain three years from now.

2. Performance

For content screens, forms, lists and payments, Flutter is fast enough as long as you follow its performance best practices: lazy lists, properly sized images, few rebuilds. Native keeps the edge for real-time processing (camera, audio, video) and animations tightly bound to the system. For a 3D game, look at Unity instead.

3. Platform APIs

This is the most underrated criterion. Flutter covers the everyday needs through plugins, but a recent or specialized feature means depending on a third-party plugin or writing native code through platform channels:

const channel = MethodChannel('com.example.app/battery');
final level = await channel.invokeMethod<int>('level');

You then write the Kotlin and Swift sides too. If you expect many of these (home-screen widgets, Live Activities, advanced Bluetooth, CarPlay), native is simpler.

4. Hiring and cost

Swift and Kotlin developers are more plentiful, but a good mobile developer picks up Dart in a few weeks. I won't quote a savings percentage; it depends too much on the project. Flutter cuts the work on shared logic and UI; it doesn't remove publishing to two stores or yearly platform upgrades.

When each option wins

SituationUsual choice
Small team, iOS and Android, custom UIFlutter
Proof of concept on two platformsFlutter
iOS only, deep Apple integrationSwift / SwiftUI
Real-time camera, audio or sensorsNative
Native teams already in placeNative
2D or 3D gameUnity

Classic pitfall: choosing Flutter to maintain one codebase, then piling on native modules. You end up with three codebases. Before adding a plugin, check its last commit date and whether it supports both platforms.

Conclusion

There is no universal winner. For most small teams targeting iOS and Android on a limited budget, Flutter is a good default. If the product is deeply tied to the platform or your native teams exist, stay native. Settle it with a one-week prototype of the riskiest feature, not the home screen.
