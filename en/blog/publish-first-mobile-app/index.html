Publishing your first mobile app on Google Play and the App Store

By Kallistesoft · November 28, 2026 · 3 min read

Writing the app is half the work. The other half, which surprises almost everyone, is publishing: accounts, signing, testing, the listing, privacy and review. Here is the path as I follow it, with the pitfalls that cost days.

1. Developer accounts

You need one account per store: a one-time fee at Google Play, an annual fee for the Apple Developer Program. Check current amounts on the official sites. Decide up front between a personal and an organization account: a company must provide identity documents (a D-U-N-S number for Apple), which can take days. Do it first, with a shared company email address.

2. App ID and signing

The application ID (for example com.example.myapp) is permanent once published. On Android, with Play App Signing, Google holds the final key and you keep an upload key; back it up off your computer and never commit it to Git (see the signing documentation):

keytool -genkey -v -keystore upload-keystore.jks \
  -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000 -alias upload
flutter build appbundle --release

On iOS, enable Xcode's automatic signing for a first app. Flutter's guides for Android and iOS detail each step.

3. Test before you publish

Don't submit straight to production. Google Play has test tracks (internal, closed, open); new personal accounts may need a closed test with a minimum number of testers before production, so check the current rules in Play Console. On Apple's side, TestFlight sends builds to internal testers (no review) and external testers (short review). Test the production build on real devices: minification, permissions and server configuration sometimes behave differently than in development.

4. Listing, privacy and declarations

Prepare the name, description, icon and screenshots of the real app, in each language your users speak. Above all you need a public privacy policy at a stable URL that matches what the app does, third-party libraries included (analytics, crash reporting, ads). Then fill in Google Play's "Data safety" section and Apple's App Privacy details, SDK by SDK. If users can create an account, provide a way to delete it too.

5. Review pitfalls

  • No demo account when login is required.
  • Crash on first launch: test a clean install, offline.
  • "Coming soon" features: Apple rejects what looks unfinished.
  • In-app purchases not approved before submission.
  • Inconsistent declarations: "no data collected", yet an analytics SDK is included.

Read Apple's review guidelines once. A review takes from hours to several days: don't set a firm launch date before the first approval.

Conclusion

Nothing here is hard, but all of it takes time. Publishing a first app is mostly preparation: open accounts early, back up your keys, test the real build and keep your declarations accurate. The first upload is the slowest; later ones become routine.
